AAVE and stereotypes — homework

Vocabulary, grammar and a short writing task from our lesson on stereotypes and African American Vernacular English.

Before you start. AAVE (African American Vernacular English) is a legitimate dialect of English with its own consistent grammar. It is not "broken" or "lazy" English. Misunderstanding it is one of the ways language differences turn into prejudice.

Each task has three buttons: Check (marks every answer), Show answers (fills them in, no score) and Try again. At the end, press Copy my results and paste the report into the chat.

Cheat sheet: key words and AAVE features
WordPronunciation (AmE)Meaning
stereotype/ˈster.i.ə.taɪp/an oversimplified, widely held belief about a group
prejudice/ˈpredʒ.ə.dɪs/a negative opinion about a group formed without real knowledge
discrimination/dɪˌskrɪm.ɪˈneɪ.ʃən/unfair treatment of people because of their group
bias/ˈbaɪ.əs/a tendency to favor or dislike someone, often unconsciously
marginalization/ˌmɑːr.dʒɪ.nə.laɪˈzeɪ.ʃən/pushing a group to the edges of society, denying it power
caricature/ˈker.ɪ.kə.tʃʊr/an exaggerated, distorted picture of a group, used for mockery
