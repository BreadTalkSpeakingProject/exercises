Conditionals and Qualifying Claims

Choose the time relationship

Second conditional
Imagined now or future · if + past simple → would + base verb
Third conditional
Past choice → past result · if + had + past participle → would have + past participle
Mixed conditional
Past choice → present result · if + had + past participle → would + base verb

1. Match the time relationship

Choose the type of conditional for each sentence.

2. Complete the conditionals

Read the situation and complete both verb phrases. Use would for the result. Full forms and contractions both count. For example, either had finished or I’d finished is accepted when the subject is I.

  1. You didn’t join a conversation group last month. You still feel nervous speaking. Imagine the effect of joining.

    If I (join) the conversation group last month, I (feel) more confident now.

  2. You lost your study notes when your laptop stopped working. Imagine you had a backup.

    If I (back up) my notes before my laptop stopped working, I (recover) them afterwards.

  3. The library closes before you finish work. Imagine it stays open later.

    If it (stay) open later, I (study) there after work.

  4. You gave away your bike, so you walk to work now. Imagine you still had it.

    If I (not give away) my bike, I probably (not be walking) to work now.

  5. You don’t have a quiet room where you can practise the piano.

    If I (have) a quiet room, I (practise) the piano every day.

  6. You took the wrong bus and missed the start of a workshop. Imagine catching the right bus.

    If I (take) the right bus, I (arrive) before the workshop started.

3. Qualify a claim

Write your own version, then compare it with the suggested answer.

  1. Limit a generalisation

    Studying with a friend always improves your results.

    Show suggested answer

    Studying with a friend may improve your results because you can explain difficult ideas to each other.

    May makes the improvement a possibility. The reason shows how studying together could help.

  2. Add a condition

    Recorded lessons make it easy for everyone to catch up.

    Show suggested answer

    Recorded lessons can help students catch up as long as the sound is clear enough to follow.

    Clear sound is a specific condition students need in order to follow the recording.

  3. Acknowledge a benefit

    Schools should replace all paper textbooks with digital ones.

    Show suggested answer

    Although digital textbooks may be easier to carry, schools should keep some paper copies available for students who prefer reading on paper.

    The first clause acknowledges a benefit. The main clause gives a specific recommendation.

  4. Link a consequence

    A community centre has moved its classes to the evening. This could help people who work during the day attend.

    Show suggested answer

    A community centre has moved its classes to the evening, which could help people who work during the day attend.

    Which refers to moving the classes to the evening. Could shows that the consequence is possible.
